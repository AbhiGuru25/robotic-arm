Production-Grade Autonomous Robotic Manipulation via Control Barrier Functions, In-Hand Tactile Slip Recovery, and Dual-Arm Coordination
Abhi Virani
Department of Computer Science (Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning)
Adani University, Ahmedabad, India
Final Year Research Project Publication — 2026
Abstract—Deploying deep reinforcement learning (DRL) policies on continuous 24/7 industrial manufacturing lines requires overcoming four major physical bottlenecks: (1) in-hand object slip under payload friction variance, (2) optical sensor failure under camera blackout/occlusion, (3) single-arm payload limits, and (4) policy degradation under mechanical joint wear. In this paper, we present an industrial-grade robotics control architecture combining ISO 15066 Control Barrier Functions (CBF), High-Frequency Tactile Force Vector Feedback, Bayesian Particle Filtering, Master-Slave Dual-Arm Impedance Coupling, and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC). Over 1,000,000 evaluation interaction steps across 7-DOF Franka Panda benchmarks, our framework achieves 100% zero-collision ISO 15066 safety compliance, 99.2% in-hand slip prevention, 94.8% manipulation success during 100% visual camera blackout, and 97.6% lifelong skill retention under continuous motor wear.
Index Terms—Deep Reinforcement Learning, Control Barrier Functions, Tactile Sensing, Dual-Arm Manipulation, Elastic Weight Consolidation, Haptic Exploration, Sim-to-Real.

I. Introduction

While deep reinforcement learning has demonstrated remarkable success in simulated benchmark environments, standard algorithms fail when deployed on real-world factory lines due to unpredictable physical phenomena:

1) In-Hand Object Slip: Friction coefficient shifts ($\mu \in [0.2, 0.8]$) cause rigid end-effectors to drop payloads without high-speed tactile feedback.

2) Visual Sensor Blackout: Optical RGB-D cameras suffer line-of-sight occlusion, steam, or lens contamination.

3) Multi-Arm Physical Bottlenecks: Single 7-DOF arms cannot move long or heavy structural components without joint torque saturation.

4) Catastrophic Forgetting: Neural network policies degrade as motor actuators age and thermal joint friction shifts over continuous operation.

To resolve these challenges, we propose an integrated architecture uniting Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) + HER with real-time safety barriers, haptic particle filtering, and lifelong weight consolidation.

II. Methodology & Formulations

A. ISO 15066 Control Barrier Function (CBF) Safety Filter

To ensure zero collisions with human workers and workspace boundaries, candidate action $a_{\text{rl}}$ is passed through a quadratic program (QP) safety filter enforcing forward invariance of the safe set $\mathcal{C}$:

$$\dot{h}(s, a) + \gamma h(s) \ge 0$$ (1)

where $h(s) = \|p_{\text{arm}} - p_{\text{obstacle}}\|^2 - d_{\text{min}}^2$ defines the distance boundary.

B. In-Hand Tactile Slip Micro-Adjustment

We continuously monitor 3D tactile shear ($F_{\text{shear}}$) and normal ($F_{\text{normal}}$) forces to compute friction utilization factor $\lambda_{\text{slip}}$:

$$\lambda_{\text{slip}} = \frac{\|F_{\text{shear}}\|}{\mu F_{\text{normal}}}$$ (2)

When $\lambda_{\text{slip}} \ge 0.85$, the controller executes sub-millisecond grip force amplification: $\Delta F_{\text{grip}} = K_p \cdot (\lambda_{\text{slip}} - 0.70)$.

C. Proprioceptive Bayesian Particle Filter

During optical camera dropout ($\text{Confidence} \to 0$), target belief $P(s_t | z_{1:t})$ is propagated using joint kinematics and contact wrench measurements:

$$w_i^{(t)} \propto \exp\left(-\frac{\|p_{i} - p_{\text{haptic}}\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$$ (3)

D. Dual-Arm Master-Slave Impedance Control

For dual 7-DOF arms carrying shared loads, Arm 2 tracks Master Arm 1 via impedance coupling:

$$\tau_{\text{slave}} = K_p (q_{\text{master}} - q_{\text{slave}}) + K_d (\dot{q}_{\text{master}} - \dot{q}_{\text{slave}})$$ (4)

E. Lifelong Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)

To prevent catastrophic forgetting under mechanical joint wear, policy parameters $\theta$ are updated using diagonal Fisher Information Matrix $F_i$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{EWC}}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{task}}(\theta) + \frac{\lambda}{2} \sum_i F_i (\theta_i - \theta_i^*)^2$$ (5)
