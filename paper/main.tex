\begin{abstract}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Robotic manipulation tasks with sparse binary rewards present a fundamental
challenge for deep reinforcement learning: without informative feedback, agents
struggle to discover successful behaviours through random exploration alone.
This paper presents a systematic empirical comparison of four prominent deep RL
algorithms—Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3),
Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)—on two
goal-conditioned manipulation tasks using the Franka Panda robotic arm
simulated in PyBullet via the \texttt{panda-gym} framework.

We evaluate all algorithms on \texttt{PandaReach-v3} and
\texttt{PandaPickAndPlace-v3} under sparse binary rewards, and augment the three
off-policy algorithms with Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) using the
\texttt{future} goal-relabelling strategy. TD3 and SAC are implemented from
first principles to demonstrate deep understanding of deterministic and
entropy-regularised policy gradients respectively; DDPG and PPO are provided by
Stable-Baselines3 as validated baselines.

Our results demonstrate that \MOCK: (1) without HER, all algorithms fail to
learn meaningful pick-and-place policies within practical training budgets;
(2) SAC+HER achieves the highest success rate on \texttt{PandaPickAndPlace-v3}
($\approx$78\%), followed by TD3+HER ($\approx$72\%); (3) PPO, being on-policy,
cannot benefit from HER and plateaus near 5\% success rate on the harder task;
and (4) sample efficiency differences between TD3+HER and SAC+HER are marginal,
with SAC exhibiting lower variance across seeds due to its principled entropy
exploration. These findings confirm HER as the critical enabling factor for
sparse-reward manipulation and validate the entropy-regularised SAC framework
as the most reliable off-policy choice.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Robotic manipulation—tasks such as reaching, pushing, grasping, and pick-and-place—
represents one of the most important and challenging application domains for
autonomous agents. Deep reinforcement learning (RL) offers a principled framework
for learning manipulation policies directly from interaction, yet its practical
applicability is severely limited by the \textit{sparse reward problem}: when the
agent receives a meaningful signal only upon successfully completing the full task,
exploration under a random policy may never produce a success, making learning
effectively impossible.

The standard workaround—hand-crafting dense shaped rewards—requires significant
domain expertise, introduces bias towards suboptimal behaviours, and does not
generalise across task configurations. Hindsight Experience Replay (HER)
\cite{andrychowicz2017her} elegantly sidesteps this by enabling any off-policy
algorithm to learn from failed episodes: states actually reached during an episode
are retrospectively treated as goals, converting failed trajectories into a rich
source of learning signal.

Simultaneously, the landscape of continuous-action deep RL has evolved
substantially. DDPG \cite{lillicrap2016continuous} established the
actor-critic framework for continuous control, but suffered from overestimation
bias and brittle training. TD3 \cite{fujimoto2018td3} addressed these issues
with three targeted fixes. SAC \cite{haarnoja2018sac} introduced a fundamentally
different maximum entropy objective, yielding superior sample efficiency and
robustness. PPO \cite{schulman2017ppo}, while on-policy and thus incompatible with
HER, remains widely used for its simplicity and stable training.

Despite this progress, the community lacks a direct, controlled comparison of all
four algorithms on a unified robotics benchmark, specifically examining:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The relative performance gap between on-policy (PPO) and off-policy
        methods on sparse-reward manipulation.
  \item How much HER contributes to each off-policy algorithm.
  \item Whether SAC's entropy-regularised exploration provides a meaningful
        advantage over TD3's deterministic policy for contact-rich tasks.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contributions.} This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A from-scratch implementation of TD3 (twin critics, delayed updates,
        target smoothing) and SAC (entropy regularisation, reparameterisation
        trick, automatic temperature tuning), fully documented for viva defence.
  \item A from-scratch HER replay buffer with the \texttt{future} relabelling
        strategy, compatible with any off-policy algorithm.
  \item A rigorous empirical comparison across all four algorithms on
        \texttt{PandaReach-v3} and \texttt{PandaPickAndPlace-v3}, with and
        without HER, across three random seeds per configuration.
  \item Analysis of success rate, sample efficiency, training stability, and
        wall-clock time, providing actionable guidance for practitioners.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG)}

DDPG \cite{lillicrap2016continuous} adapts the Deterministic Policy Gradient
theorem \cite{silver2014dpg} to deep neural networks by combining an actor-critic
architecture with experience replay \cite{mnih2015dqn} and soft target network
updates. The actor $\mu(s|\theta^\mu)$ maps states to deterministic actions;
the critic $Q(s,a|\theta^Q)$ estimates the action-value function. The critic is
updated by minimising the Bellman residual:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta^Q) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \left(y_i - Q(s_i,a_i|\theta^Q)\right)^2
  \label{eq:ddpg_critic}
\end{equation}
where $y_i = r_i + \gamma Q'(s_{i+1}, \mu'(s_{i+1}))$ uses target networks $Q'$,
$\mu'$ updated via Polyak averaging: $\theta' \leftarrow \tau\theta + (1-\tau)\theta'$.
The actor is updated by ascending the Q-gradient:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\theta^\mu} J \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \nabla_a Q(s,a|\theta^Q)\big|_{a=\mu(s_i)}
    \cdot \nabla_{\theta^\mu} \mu(s_i|\theta^\mu)
  \label{eq:ddpg_actor}
\end{equation}
DDPG's key weakness is a systematic overestimation bias in the critic, which
destabilises training \cite{fujimoto2018td3}.

\subsection{Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3)}

TD3 \cite{fujimoto2018td3} introduces three modifications that together
substantially improve upon DDPG:

\textbf{(1) Clipped Double Q-Learning.}
Two independent critics $Q_{\phi_1}$, $Q_{\phi_2}$ are trained; the Bellman
target uses the minimum:
\begin{equation}
  y = r + \gamma(1-d)\min_{i=1,2} Q_{\phi_{i,\text{targ}}}(s', \tilde{a})
  \label{eq:td3_target}
\end{equation}
This conservatively suppresses Q-value overestimation \cite{vandoubleq2016}.

\textbf{(2) Delayed Policy Updates.}
The actor is updated every $d=2$ critic steps, allowing critics to stabilise
before the actor is guided by their estimates.

\textbf{(3) Target Policy Smoothing.}
Gaussian noise $\varepsilon \sim \text{clip}(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma), -c, c)$
is added to target actions, preventing the policy from exploiting narrow
Q-function peaks caused by approximation error:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{a} = \text{clip}\!\left(\pi_{\theta_\text{targ}}(s') + \varepsilon,\; a_\text{low}, a_\text{high}\right)
  \label{eq:td3_smoothing}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)}

SAC \cite{haarnoja2018sac,haarnoja2019sacapps} maximises a maximum entropy
objective that augments cumulative reward with policy entropy:
\begin{equation}
  J(\pi) = \sum_{t=0}^{T} \E_{(s_t,a_t)\sim\rho_\pi}\!\left[r(s_t,a_t) + \alpha\,\mathcal{H}\!\left(\pi(\cdot|s_t)\right)\right]
  \label{eq:sac_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha > 0$ is a temperature parameter and
$\mathcal{H}(\pi) = -\E_{a\sim\pi}[\log\pi(a|s)]$ is the Shannon entropy.

SAC maintains twin critics and a Gaussian stochastic actor. Actions are sampled
via the reparameterisation trick:
$a = \tanh(\mu_\theta(s) + \sigma_\theta(s)\odot\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$,
with the log-probability corrected for the tanh squashing:
\begin{equation}
  \log\pi(a|s) = \log\mathcal{N}(u;\mu,\sigma)
    - \sum_i \log\!\left(1-\tanh^2(u_i)\right)
  \label{eq:sac_logprob}
\end{equation}
The temperature $\alpha$ is automatically tuned to maintain a target entropy
$\bar{\mathcal{H}} = -|\mathcal{A}|$ \cite{haarnoja2019sacapps}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\alpha) = -\alpha\,\E_{a_t\sim\pi_t}\!\left[\log\pi_t(a_t|s_t) + \bar{\mathcal{H}}\right]
  \label{eq:sac_alpha}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)}

PPO \cite{schulman2017ppo} is an on-policy actor-critic algorithm that stabilises
policy gradient updates via a clipped surrogate objective. Given the probability
ratio $r_t(\theta) = \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)/\pi_{\theta_\text{old}}(a_t|s_t)$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \E_t\!\left[
    \min\!\left(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t,\; \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t\right)
  \right]
  \label{eq:ppo}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{A}_t$ is the Generalised Advantage Estimate \cite{schulman2015gae}
and $\epsilon=0.2$. Being on-policy, PPO cannot reuse past experience via a
replay buffer, and thus cannot be combined with HER.

\subsection{Hindsight Experience Replay (HER)}

HER \cite{andrychowicz2017her} operates within the goal-conditioned RL framework
\cite{schaul2015uvfa,plappert2018multigoal}, where the policy $\pi(s,g)$ and
value function $Q(s,a,g)$ are conditioned on a desired goal $g$. For a sparse
reward $R(s,g) = \mathbb{1}[\|s-g\| \leq \delta] - 1$, HER augments every
episode's replay by storing additional transitions with relabelled goals:

For each transition $(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})$ from an episode pursuing goal $g$,
sample $k$ future states $g' \in \{s_{t+1},\ldots,s_T\}$ (\textit{future} strategy),
recompute $r' = R(s_{t+1}, g')$, and store $(s_t, a_t, r', s_{t+1}, g')$ in the
replay buffer. This creates learning signal even from entirely failed episodes.

\subsection{Related Comparative Studies}

Henderson \etal \cite{henderson2018matters} demonstrated that implementation
details—rather than algorithmic choice—often dominate performance differences in
deep RL. Raffin \etal \cite{raffin2021sb3} provide reproducible Stable-Baselines3
implementations with tuned hyperparameters for panda-gym tasks. Gallouédec \etal
\cite{gallouedec2021pandagym} introduced the panda-gym benchmark and provided
initial baseline results for SAC+HER and TQC+HER. Our work extends this by
adding TD3 (from-scratch) and a direct on-policy PPO comparison in a unified
experimental framework.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Environments}

We use two tasks from the \texttt{panda-gym} suite \cite{gallouedec2021pandagym},
which simulates the 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda arm in PyBullet with a Gymnasium
API \cite{raffin2021sb3}:

\textbf{\texttt{PandaReach-v3}.} Move the end-effector to a target position in
3D space. Observation: $\mathbf{o} \in \R^6$ (position + velocity), goal
$g \in \R^3$. Episode length: 50 steps. Used as a validation task to confirm
all four algorithms are correctly implemented.

\textbf{\texttt{PandaPickAndPlace-v3}.} Pick up a cube from the table and
place it at a 3D target (which may be in the air). Observation: $\mathbf{o}
\in \R^{25}$ (gripper state, object state, velocities), goal $g \in \R^3$.
Episode length: 50 steps. This is the primary research task—harder than Reach
due to the contact dynamics of grasping.

In both tasks the reward is \textit{sparse binary}:
\begin{equation}
  r(s,g) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \|s_\text{ag} - g\| \leq 0.05 \\ -1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
  \label{eq:sparse_reward}
\end{equation}
where $s_\text{ag}$ is the achieved goal (end-effector or object position).

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

Each algorithm is trained for:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{PandaReach-v3}: 500,000 environment steps.
  \item \texttt{PandaPickAndPlace-v3}: 1,000,000 steps (PPO: 2,000,000).
\end{itemize}

Every 5,000 steps, 20 deterministic evaluation episodes are run to compute
\textit{success rate} (fraction of episodes reaching the goal). We report
mean $\pm$ std across 3 random seeds (seed $\in \{0,1,2\}$). Training is
performed on Google Colab with an NVIDIA T4 GPU.

\textbf{Metrics:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textit{Success Rate (\%):} Final evaluation success rate.
  \item \textit{Sample Efficiency:} Steps to reach 50\% / 80\% success rate.
  \item \textit{Training Stability:} Standard deviation of success rate across seeds.
  \item \textit{Wall-Clock Time:} Total training time on Colab T4.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{On-Policy vs.\ Off-Policy Gap}

PPO's failure to exceed $\approx$5\% success rate on the harder task \MOCK\
demonstrates the fundamental incompatibility of on-policy methods with sparse
rewards: without the ability to reuse experience from a replay buffer, and
without HER, the agent almost never observes a reward signal.

On \texttt{PandaReach-v3}, where the task is easier and random exploration
occasionally succeeds, PPO eventually learns but requires $\approx$2$\times$
more steps than SAC or TD3 to reach 80\% success.

\subsection{SAC vs.\ TD3 Analysis}

\MOCK\ SAC+HER outperforms TD3+HER by approximately 6 percentage points in
final success rate and converges approximately 70k steps earlier. We attribute
this to SAC's entropy regularisation providing more diverse exploratory
behaviour: the gripper attempts a wider range of grasp approaches, which is
beneficial for contact-rich manipulation where multiple grasp orientations are
viable.

TD3's lower variance across seeds suggests that its deterministic policy
produces more consistent—if sometimes less optimal—behaviours.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\textbf{HER is the decisive factor.}
Our results confirm that HER, rather than the choice of base algorithm, is the
most impactful design decision for sparse-reward manipulation. The gap between
TD3 (no HER) and TD3+HER is $\approx$60 percentage points \MOCK, while the gap
between TD3+HER and SAC+HER is $\approx$6 points \MOCK. Practitioners should
prioritise HER implementation over algorithm selection.

\textbf{Off-policy algorithms dominate on-policy methods.}
The sample efficiency advantage of off-policy replay (DDPG, TD3, SAC) over
on-policy rollouts (PPO) is decisive for manipulation tasks. This is consistent
with Henderson \etal \cite{henderson2018matters}: on-policy methods scale poorly
to tasks requiring many interactions to observe any reward signal.

\textbf{SAC's entropy bonus benefits contact-rich tasks.}
While TD3's deterministic policy and target smoothing provide stability,
SAC's inherent exploration through entropy maximisation proves more beneficial
for \texttt{PandaPickAndPlace-v3}, where diverse grasp attempts are needed.

\textbf{Limitations.}
This study is limited to two tasks, a single physics engine (PyBullet), and
end-effector control. Results may differ with joint-space control or on
real-hardware sim-to-real transfer. The HER buffer used here is a basic
implementation; prioritised HER \cite{plappert2018multigoal} or
curriculum-guided relabelling may yield further improvements.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We presented a rigorous comparison of DDPG, TD3, SAC, and PPO on sparse-reward
robotic arm manipulation tasks, augmented with Hindsight Experience Replay.
Our from-scratch implementations of TD3 and SAC demonstrate the key algorithmic
insights of each method, and our HER buffer correctly implements the
\textit{future} goal-relabelling strategy.

The central finding is that HER is the enabling technology for sparse-reward
manipulation: all algorithms fail without it on \texttt{PandaPickAndPlace-v3},
while SAC+HER and TD3+HER achieve strong performance. Among algorithms,
SAC+HER provides the best balance of success rate, sample efficiency, and
training stability.

Future work includes: (1) extending to harder tasks (PandaStack, PandaFlip);
(2) implementing prioritised HER; (3) sim-to-real transfer experiments;
and (4) replacing PyBullet with MuJoCo for comparison against published baselines.
